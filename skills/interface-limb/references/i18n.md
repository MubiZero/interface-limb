## Plurals, gender and case

- Plural rules differ: English has 2 forms, Russian 3 (1 позиция, 2 позиции, 5 позиций), Arabic 6. Use the
  platform's plural machinery (ICU MessageFormat, Flutter `intl` plural, Android `plurals`), never `n + " item(s)"`.
- Gender and grammatical case change words around a name or a noun; a template that inserts a noun into a sentence
  usually breaks in a case-rich language («от {name}» needs the genitive).

## Names inside sentences

Write sentences so names stand in the nominative and need no agreement: «Daler handed in 145.00», «Courier: Daler»,
«Order 737 · Daler», not «received from Daler» (case) or «he handed in» (gender). Avoid gendered pronouns for people
whose gender the product does not know.

## Text expansion and scripts

- German and Russian run 20–35 % longer than English; short labels can double. Leave room, let buttons grow, test the
  longest language at the narrowest width.
- Scripts differ in height (Arabic, Thai, Devanagari need more line height); Tajik and Kazakh Cyrillic have letters
  (ҳ, қ, ӯ, ғ, ҷ, ӣ) that many fonts lack — check the font covers them.
- Do not truncate names mid-word in languages without spaces; use the platform's line breaking.

## Right-to-left

Mirror the layout (start/end, not left/right, in all spacing and alignment tokens), mirror directional icons (back,
forward, progress), keep numbers, phone numbers and media controls left-to-right. Test with real RTL content.

## Numbers, money, dates, phones

- Format with the locale (`Intl.NumberFormat`, `NumberFormat` in Flutter): thousands separator (a space in Russian,
  a comma in English), decimal mark, currency symbol and its position («142 с.», «$142»), minus sign.
- Dates and times in the locale's order and 12/24 h; relative times («4 min ago») from the platform.
- Phone numbers with the country's grouping; inputs accept any grouping and normalize.

## One string, one meaning

One concept has one key and one translation everywhere; do not reuse a key because the English words match in one
place (the same English word may need different translations in two contexts). Keep a glossary of domain terms per
language, agreed with someone who works in that language.

## Fonts for every language

The chosen family must cover every language the product ships; fall back deliberately (a matching system font), not to
whatever the OS picks.

## Testing

Pseudo-localization (accented, 35 % longer strings) catches clipping and hard-coded strings before translation exists.
Then each shipped language at the narrowest width, with real names and amounts. A test that every shipped key is used
and every used key exists in every language keeps the files honest.
